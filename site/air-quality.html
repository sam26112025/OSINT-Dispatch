Understanding the AQI Scale

The Air Quality Index (AQI) translates complex air pollution data into a single, easy-to-understand number. It reflects the concentration of key pollutants — primarily fine particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10), ground-level ozone, nitrogen dioxide, sulphur dioxide, and carbon monoxide. The higher the AQI, the greater the level of air pollution and the associated health concern.

Why Air Quality Matters in India

India is home to several of the world's most polluted cities, and air quality is a daily concern for hundreds of millions of people. During winter months, cities across the northern plains — including New Delhi, Ghaziabad, and Noida — frequently record AQI values in the Very Unhealthy and Hazardous ranges. Real-time AQI awareness helps individuals decide when to limit outdoor activity, wear protective masks, or use air purifiers. For organisations and investigators working long hours, monitoring local air quality is a practical health and wellbeing measure.

PM2.5 and PM10 Explained

Particulate matter is among the most health-significant pollutants. PM10 refers to particles up to 10 micrometres in diameter, which can irritate the airways. PM2.5 — fine particles under 2.5 micrometres — is far more dangerous, penetrating deep into the lungs and entering the bloodstream, where it contributes to respiratory and cardiovascular disease. Because of this, PM2.5 is often the dominant factor in determining a city's AQI, particularly in India's urban centres.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good AQI level?
An AQI between 0 and 50 is Good, meaning air quality is satisfactory with little or no health risk. Values above 100 become increasingly unhealthy, especially for sensitive groups.

What is the AQI in Delhi today?
This page shows the live AQI for New Delhi by default, updated continuously. You can search any other city, state, or country to see its current air quality.

What does PM2.5 mean?
PM2.5 is fine particulate matter under 2.5 micrometres. It penetrates deep into the lungs and bloodstream, making it one of the most health-significant pollutants and a key driver of AQI.

How is the AQI calculated?
The AQI converts pollutant concentrations such as PM2.5, PM10, ozone, and nitrogen dioxide into a single standardised scale. The pollutant with the worst sub-index sets the overall AQI.
