> DATA BREACH CHECK_

Find out if your email appears in known data breaches, or if a password has been exposed. Passwords are checked using k-anonymity — your password never leaves your browser.

🔒 Password Exposure

Checks if a password appears in known breach corpora. Uses k-anonymity (HIBP Pwned Passwords) — only the first 5 chars of the SHA-1 hash are sent. The password itself is never transmitted.

📧 Email Exposure

Checks if an email address appears in known data breaches and shows what data was exposed. Powered by the free XposedOrNot API. Check your own address.
Privacy: This tool runs entirely in your browser. We do not store, log, or transmit your queries to IGRS servers. Password checks use k-anonymity (only a partial hash leaves your device). Email checks send the address you enter directly to the XposedOrNot API for lookup. Please check only your own email or password.

Why check for breach exposure?

When a service you use is breached, your credentials can end up in public data dumps traded on forums and the dark web. Attackers then reuse those email-password pairs against other sites (credential stuffing). Knowing where you've been exposed lets you rotate passwords and enable multi-factor authentication before that data is weaponised against you.

How the password check stays private

Your password is hashed with SHA-1 inside your browser. Only the first five characters of that hash are sent to the breach database, which returns every hash suffix sharing that prefix. The final match happens locally. The breach service never sees your password or its full hash — this is the k-anonymity model.

If you find you've been exposed

  • Change the password on the affected service immediately, and anywhere you reused it.
  • Switch to unique passwords per site, ideally via a password manager.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) on email and financial accounts first.
  • Watch for phishing that references the breached service or leaked details.
