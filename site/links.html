Link Shortening, QR Codes and Operational Security

Short links and QR codes are convenient, but in an investigative and security context they carry risk as well as utility. This tool lets you create clean, branded short links for sharing resources with a team or generating a QR code for print and field use — while keeping you mindful of how the same mechanisms are abused by adversaries.

Why shortened links deserve caution

A shortened URL hides its destination, which is precisely why it is a favoured vector in phishing and fraud campaigns. As a matter of routine, never open an unfamiliar short link directly: expand it first using a preview or scanning service, and treat any link delivered with urgency — a payment, a KYC update, a parcel notice — as suspect until verified. The same advice applies to QR codes, which are simply links in visual form and can be swapped or overlaid in the physical world.

Preserving links as evidence

When a link is part of a case, record the full destination URL, the date you observed it, and a capture of where it led — not merely the shortened form, which may expire or be repointed. A preserved, timestamped record of the original resource is what makes a finding reproducible if it later supports a complaint to the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal or an FIR.
