Blockchain investigation for Indian cyber cells

Cryptocurrency features in a growing share of fraud, extortion, and money-laundering cases in India. Because the Bitcoin and Ethereum ledgers are public, investigators can follow the movement of funds across wallets without any special access. This tool reads that public data and presents a counterparty breakdown — the addresses that funded a wallet and the addresses it paid — so analysts can build the breadcrumb trail that underpins a financial investigation.

From on-chain lead to lawful attribution

On-chain analysis identifies wallets and flows, but it does not name a person. Linking an address to a real identity requires KYC or subscriber records from a regulated exchange, obtained through due legal process under Section 94 BNSS, with electronic evidence certified under Section 63 BSA. Cryptocurrency laundering also engages the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, Sections 3 and 4. Used together, open-source on-chain tracing and lawful records turn a single wallet address into a defensible investigation.
