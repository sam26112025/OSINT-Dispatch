Working from the IGRS OSINT Workstation

The workstation brings the first moves of an investigation into a single pane. When you enter an indicator, the lookup panel detects whether it is an IP address, a domain, a file hash, or a CVE identifier, and generates direct queries against the appropriate sources — VirusTotal, Shodan, AbuseIPDB, and others — so you can pivot in seconds rather than opening each service by hand.

Designed around how investigations actually run

The inline utilities cover the small, repeated tasks that interrupt an analyst’s flow: hashing and encoding, CIDR scoping, password and regex checks tuned to Indian patterns, and timestamp conversion. The IST clock and time-zone strip exist because correlating logs across regions is a routine source of error; having local and reference times side by side reduces mistakes when you reconstruct a sequence of events.

India quick-reference, always at hand

The workstation keeps the contacts and provisions an Indian investigator reaches for most often within reach — the 1930 cyber-fraud helpline, CERT-In for incident reporting, and the relevant sections of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. The intended workflow is simple and defensible: begin with a single indicator, pivot across independent sources to confirm it, and document each step as you go so that the result can be reproduced and stands up to scrutiny.
