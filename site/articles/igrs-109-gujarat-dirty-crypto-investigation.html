Gujarat ₹226 Crore Dirty Crypto Case: How Investigators Traced USDT from Dark Web to Terror Financing

By Manuj Kumar Pandey 2 July 2026 Crypto Intelligence Terror Financing USDT TRC-20 OFAC SDN

⚠ Disclaimer: This article is based entirely on publicly available information from official Gujarat CID press statements, PTI/IANS wire reports, and OFAC public SDN records. No confidential investigation data is referenced. Wallet addresses cited are from published OFAC/OpenSanctions public lists. This analysis is for investigative education and methodology demonstration only.

On 19 May 2026, Gujarat CID Crime's Cyber Centre of Excellence (CCoE) announced the arrest of nine individuals across Ahmedabad, Mumbai, and Karnal — dismantling what SP Rajdeepsinh Zala called a network of "dirty crypto." The total transaction volume traced: ₹226.54 crore ($23.96 million) across USDT stablecoins, Monero (XMR), peer-to-peer exchanges, and hawala channels — with links stretching from a dark web narcotics marketplace to designated terrorist organisations including Hamas, Houthi (Ansar Allah), and IRGC-QF.

This article reconstructs the investigative methodology that Gujarat CCoE used, and demonstrates how Indian investigators can apply the same approach using the IGRS Crypto Investigation Suite — a free, no-login tool built specifically for Indian law enforcement and cybercrime units.

The Money Trail: How Dirty Crypto Flows

The syndicate used a layering method common across India's crypto fraud-to-terror pipeline. Understanding this flow is essential for investigators building a transaction graph:

LAYER 1 — COLLECTION
Cyber fraud victims → mule bank accounts

Victims of 935+ registered cyber fraud cases transferred money to mule bank accounts controlled by the syndicate across India. This is the primary fiat entry point.

LAYER 2 — CRYPTO CONVERSION
Mule accounts → P2P crypto → USDT (TRC-20)

Cash from mule accounts was converted into USDT via peer-to-peer (P2P) cryptocurrency trading accounts. USDT on TRC-20 (Tron blockchain) was chosen for its speed, low fees, and near-instant settlement. This is the most common off-ramp in Indian cybercrime cases — and the most traceable.

LAYER 3 — OBFUSCATION
USDT rotation across wallets + Monero conversion

Funds were rotated internally across multiple wallets linked to the nine accused. Some flows were converted to Monero (XMR) — a privacy coin with ring signature anonymisation — making the Monero leg significantly harder to trace. Gujarat CCoE recovered two Monero wallets worth ₹2 crore from accused. XMR was used specifically for the dark web narcotics payments to ARTEMISLAB.CC.

LAYER 4 — TERROR ROUTING
USDT → OFAC-sanctioned wallets → international criminal networks

Cleaned USDT was routed into wallets associated with internationally sanctioned entities: Hamas (via Al-Kahira Gaza front, wallet frozen by Israel NBCTF in 2025), Ansar Allah (Houthi), IRGC-QF, and Russia's Garantex exchange. These final destinations are what triggered the terror financing sections under BNS.

LAYER 5 — CASH OUT
USDT → Hawala → INR cash in India

Simultaneously, a portion of USDT was converted back to Indian rupees through hawala networks (angadia channels). Molani in Ahmedabad collected the cash and distributed it to Ansari's network. This dual-direction flow — exporting dirty crypto internationally while importing clean cash domestically — is the hallmark of the India-Dubai-UK narco-finance triangle.

The OFAC Link: Garantex and the Gujarat Case

Gujarat CCoE investigators cross-referenced suspect wallet transaction histories against OFAC's Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list. They found that wallets belonging to Dubai-based absconding accused Mohammed Zuber Popatiya had received funds from wallets linked to Al-Kahira, a Gaza-based entity designated by Israel's National Bureau for Counter Terror Financing as a Hamas financing front. Those wallets had already been frozen by Israel NBCTF in 2025 via court order.

Additionally, investigators confirmed inbound transactions from entities sanctioned by OFAC: Garantex (the Russia-based exchange sanctioned and shut down in March 2025) and wallets linked to the Ilan Shor network (Moldova-based money laundering cluster).

Investigative significance: The OFAC SDN connection is what transformed this from a cyber fraud/PMLA case into a terror financing case under S.111(2)(B) BNS. Without blockchain tracing of the international wallet linkages, those sections could not have been invoked.

What Made This Investigation Successful

The Gujarat CCoE's approach demonstrates several principles that every cyber investigator tracing crypto should internalise:

1. Start from the blockchain, not the suspect

The investigation began with a suspicious blockchain transaction flagged on ARTEMISLAB.CC — not with a named suspect. The blockchain led to the IP address, the IP led to the wallet, the wallet led to the network. This is fundamentally different from traditional investigations and requires the investigator to treat the blockchain as a primary evidence source.

2. Cross-reference against sanctions databases

The terror financing angle was discovered only because investigators cross-referenced wallet transaction histories against OFAC and Israel NBCTF records. This step costs nothing and takes minutes — but transforms a PMLA case into a BNS S.111 case. IGRS community labels can serve this function: when any investigator tags a wallet as OFAC-sanctioned, that intelligence is visible to all subsequent investigators encountering the same wallet.

3. Follow the fiat off-ramp

Despite the sophistication of the layering, the syndicate's hawala network was the weak link. Physical cash handoffs are the most traceable element once you know the participants — and interrogation of the first arrested (Molani) exposed the entire domestic cash distribution chain. Crypto investigators should always push toward the fiat exit point.

4. USDT TRC-20 is not anonymous

Every USDT transfer on the Tron blockchain is permanently recorded and publicly accessible. The notion among criminals that USDT is "untraceable" is false. What offers partial cover is Monero — but even XMR leaves metadata that can support circumstantial investigation. The Gujarat case recovered XMR wallets from physical devices during arrest, bypassing the cryptographic anonymity entirely.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is 'dirty crypto' as used by Gujarat CID CCoE?
Dirty crypto refers to cryptocurrency derived from or used for illegal activities — including cyber fraud proceeds, dark web narcotics payments, hawala settlements, and terror financing. In the Gujarat case, approximately 30-40% of the ₹226 crore total was classified as dirty crypto. The remainder involved ostensibly legal trading, gold deals, and hawala, but even this was used to provide cover for illegal flows.
How can Indian investigators trace USDT transactions?
USDT on TRC-20 (Tron) is fully transparent on the Tron blockchain. Indian investigators can trace every transaction using the IGRS Crypto Investigation Suite at igrs.xyz/crypto-tracker.html — no paid tool or login required. The tool shows counterparty wallets, total inflow/outflow, INR equivalent, and generates LEA-ready summaries. For KYC of the exchange account, serve a S.94 BNSS notice to the Indian exchange, or initiate MLAT via CBI for foreign exchanges.
Which legal sections apply to crypto terror financing in India?
Gujarat CID registered this case under S.111(2)(B) BNS (aiding terrorist operations through financial channels), S.153 BNS (waging activities detrimental to national safety), S.61 BNS (criminal conspiracy), and S.66C/66D of the IT Act. PMLA S.3/4 applies for money laundering aspects investigated by the Enforcement Directorate. UAPA S.17 and S.40 may apply where a specifically designated terrorist organisation is the receiving entity.
What is OFAC's SDN list and why does it matter for India?
OFAC's SDN list designates individuals, entities, and wallet addresses linked to sanctioned organisations including Hamas, Houthi/Ansar Allah, IRGC-QF, and Garantex. In the Gujarat case, investigators cross-referenced suspect wallets against OFAC's list and found direct transfer links — a fact used to establish the terror financing element and secure international cooperation with Israel, US, and UK agencies.
