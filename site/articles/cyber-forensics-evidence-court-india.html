Cyber Forensics Evidence in Indian Courts — BSA 2023 Guide

Digital Evidence in Indian Courts: The New Framework

The admissibility of digital evidence in Indian courts has fundamentally changed with the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) 2023, which replaced the Indian Evidence Act 1872. For investigators and forensic experts, understanding the new requirements is essential — a technically perfect investigation can collapse in court if the evidence does not meet the BSA's standards. This guide explains how to ensure digital evidence survives judicial scrutiny.

The Section 63 Certificate

The cornerstone of digital evidence admissibility is the Section 63 certificate. It must state that the electronic record was produced by a computer used regularly for the relevant activity, that the computer was functioning properly (or that any malfunction did not affect the record), that the information was derived from data fed into the computer, and it must include the hash value of the record and the signature of the person responsible for the system.

Hash Values: Proving Integrity

The BSA 2023 makes hash values explicit. A cryptographic hash (SHA-256 is standard) computed at the time of evidence collection produces a unique fingerprint. If the evidence is altered in any way, the hash changes, immediately revealing tampering. Computing and documenting hashes at collection, and verifying them at each stage, is what proves the evidence presented in court is identical to what was originally seized.

Chain of Custody Documentation

Chain of custody documents every person who handled the evidence from seizure to court:

  • Seizure memo with device details (make, model, IMEI/serial)
  • Hash value computed at seizure
  • Evidence bag sealing with investigator signature and date
  • Forensic examination log
  • Hash comparison at examination versus seizure
  • Every transfer of custody recorded

Gaps in the chain of custody are a primary reason digital evidence is challenged and excluded.

Expert Witness Requirements

Forensic experts presenting digital evidence must establish their credibility: relevant qualifications and certifications (such as CHFI, GCFA), the validated tools used and their reliability, the methodology followed, and confirmation that examination was conducted on a forensic copy rather than the original. The expert must be able to explain the process clearly and withstand cross-examination on every step.

Common Reasons Digital Evidence Is Rejected

Understanding why evidence fails helps avoid it:

  • Missing or defective Section 63 certificate
  • Broken or undocumented chain of custody
  • No hash value or mismatched hashes
  • Examination performed on the original rather than a copy
  • Unvalidated tools or undocumented methodology
  • Use of struck-down provisions or incorrect legal framing

The Arjun Panditrao Legacy

The Supreme Court's jurisprudence, notably Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v. Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal (2020), firmly established that the certificate for electronic evidence is mandatory, not optional. The BSA 2023 continues this requirement. The practical lesson is unambiguous: oral evidence cannot substitute for the certificate, and electronic evidence without proper certification is inadmissible regardless of its content.

Practical Steps for Investigators

To ensure digital evidence holds up: compute hash values at the moment of collection, obtain the Section 63 certificate from the appropriate authority, maintain meticulous chain of custody, work only on forensic copies, use validated tools and document methodology, and apply the correct current legal provisions. Building these practices into standard operating procedure means evidence is court-ready from the moment it is collected.

Conclusion: Sound Process Equals Strong Evidence

Under the BSA 2023, the technical quality of an investigation matters only if matched by legal soundness in how evidence is handled. The Section 63 certificate, hash verification, chain of custody, and expert testimony together form the framework that transforms digital data into admissible evidence. For Indian investigators, mastering this framework is as important as the forensic skills themselves — because evidence that cannot be admitted cannot convict.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Section 63 BSA certificate for digital evidence?
Section 63 of Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023 requires a certificate accompanying electronic evidence, signed by the person in charge of the computer system. It must include the hash value of the record and certify the system was functioning properly.
Is SHA-256 hash required for digital evidence in India?
Yes, hash values (SHA-256 is the standard) are explicitly required under BSA 2023 Section 63 for electronic evidence admissibility. Hash values prove the evidence has not been tampered with since collection.
What is chain of custody for digital evidence in India?
Chain of custody documents every person who handled digital evidence — from seizure to court. It includes seizure memo, evidence bag sealing, forensic examination log, and hash verification at each stage. Gaps in chain of custody can result in evidence rejection.
