Mapping Your Organization's Internet-Facing Attack Surface with Free Tools

File No.IGRS-047/2026
SubjectAttack surface discovery methodology using Shodan, Censys, and free open-source tools
CategoryThreat Intelligence
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-04-27
Reading time5 minutes

Attackers map your internet-facing systems before they attack. Security teams should do the same — regularly. The free tools and methodology for attack surface discovery.

Attack surface discovery — the attacker's first step

Before any intrusion, attackers enumerate an organization's internet-facing infrastructure — identifying exposed systems, services, software versions, and vulnerable configurations. This reconnaissance uses the same tools defenders have: Shodan, Censys, and similar services that continuously index internet-connected systems. The difference is timing: attackers do this to exploit; defenders should do it first, and regularly.

Shodan for attack surface mapping

Shodan is a search engine for internet-connected devices and services, continuously scanning the internet and indexing what it finds — open ports, service banners, SSL certificate details, and vulnerability indicators. Search org:"Company Name" or net:IP-RANGE to see everything indexed for your organization. Review every result — forgotten test servers, misconfigured services, and exposed administrative interfaces regularly appear this way.

Censys as a complement

Censys has different scanning coverage than Shodan — systems missed by one often appear in the other. It is particularly useful for certificate-based discovery: searching for SSL certificates issued to your domain reveals every subdomain that has obtained a certificate, including infrastructure not in your asset inventory. Certificate transparency logs at crt.sh serve the same function for free.

What to investigate in scan results

Priority exposures: administrative interfaces (routers, firewalls, server management) directly exposed to the internet; remote access services (RDP 3389, SSH 22, VNC) without VPN in front; databases (MySQL 3306, MongoDB 27017, Elasticsearch 9200) with no visible authentication; old software versions with known critical CVEs; development or staging environments with production data or credentials.

Run this assessment quarterly at minimum, and immediately after any infrastructure change. Cloud environments scale dynamically in ways that manual asset management cannot track — new exposure can appear within hours of a deployment.
