VPNs in India 2026: What They Protect, What They Don't, and the New Logging Rules

File No.IGRS-039/2026
SubjectVPN privacy assessment for Indian users under CERT-In 2022 logging directions
CategoryPrivacy & Safety
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-04-03
Reading time5 minutes

CERT-In 2022 directions require VPN providers to log user data for five years. What this means for your privacy, which providers comply, and what actually protects you.

The regulatory shift

In April 2022, CERT-In issued directions requiring VPN providers operating in India to collect and retain user data — name, email, IP address, usage patterns — for five years. Providers who complied became de facto surveillance infrastructure. International providers who could not comply withdrew their Indian servers. The privacy protection a VPN offers now depends entirely on which provider you use and where their servers are located.

What a VPN still protects

Even under the new rules, a VPN encrypts traffic between your device and the VPN server, hiding activity from your ISP and local network monitors. It masks your real IP from websites you visit. For users accessing international services, a VPN with non-Indian servers exits outside CERT-In jurisdiction.

What a VPN does not protect

A VPN does not protect you from the VPN provider itself — if they log activity and receive a legal order, that data is disclosed. It does not protect against endpoint malware. Critically, it is not anonymization — it shifts trust from ISP to VPN provider, it does not eliminate it.

For genuine anonymity rather than ISP privacy, Tor remains the appropriate tool. A VPN and Tor serve different threat models and should not be conflated.

Provider selection guidance

For ISP surveillance protection, a reputable no-log international provider (Mullvad, ProtonVPN, IVPN) with documented no-log audits and servers outside India provides meaningful protection. For elevated threat models — journalists, activists, investigators — Tor over VPN plus careful account creation OPSEC provides stronger protection.
