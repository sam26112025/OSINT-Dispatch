▌ 2. Synopsis

Between December 2025 and June 2026, authorities in the Union Territory of Jammu & Kashmir (J&K) implemented a sweeping district-level prohibition on VPN services across 14 of 20 districts, citing national security threats, militant communication evasion, and coordination of anti-national activity. Issued under emergency powers (Section 163, BNSS 2023), the ban triggered the identification of over 1,100 individuals, the filing of 2 FIRs against persons with adverse terror-related antecedents, and the detention of 130+ persons on a single day (01 Jan 2026).

The crackdown originated in the Doda–Kishtwar Chenab Valley corridor — a zone of heightened militant contact — and expanded across all 10 Kashmir Valley districts and key Jammu region districts including Rajouri, Poonch, Udhampur, and Kathua. On the Pakistan-Occupied Kashmir (PoK/AJK) side, intelligence assessed from PTA regulatory filings, Freedom House Pakistan 2025 reporting, and cross-border militant communications doctrine indicates HIGH probability of VPN usage across Muzaffarabad, Neelum Valley, Rawalakot, and Mirpur — serving vectors including militant C2 communication, propaganda dissemination, and hawala/financial concealment.

▌ 8. Threat Intelligence Analysis

J&K — PRIMARY THREAT VECTORS

▶ Militant OPSEC: Device forensics on seized phones confirms terrorist VPN usage to mask identity and access encrypted apps. 2 FIRs in Budgam filed against terror-antecedent individuals.

▶ Propaganda Dissemination: VPN geo-spoofing enables upload of anti-national content from within J&K with masked local attribution, correlated with high-threat district zones.

▶ Cross-LoC C2: Encrypted VPN tunnels facilitate real-time command-and-control between handlers in PoK and active militants in Doda–Kishtwar–Pir Panjal corridor. Aligned with post-Pahalgam security timeline.

▶ Lawful Interception Evasion: VPNs directly defeat CDR-based monitoring and IMSI/cell-tower correlation — rendering J&K Police lawful interception frameworks ineffective for VPN-tunnelled traffic.

▶ Civilian Signal Noise: Majority of the 1,100+ identified are journalists, IT professionals, and students. This creates high false-positive noise masking the actual threat population within enforcement data.

PoK/AJK — ASSESSED THREAT VECTORS

▶ Militant Launchpad C2 (Muzaffarabad/Neelum): ASSESSED HIGH probability of VPN-based communications between LeT/JeM AJK infrastructure and active cells across LoC. PTA registration deliberately evaded.

▶ Information Operations: PoK-based handlers use VPNs to operate social media accounts with Indian-region IP facades — amplifying propaganda while evading platform geo-restrictions.

▶ Mirpur Diaspora Nexus: Mirpur is a historic hawala and financial support channel. VPN use assessed HIGH for concealing communications with UK-based Kashmir diaspora for fund transfers.

▶ PTI Political Cover Traffic: Mass VPN adoption driven by Pakistan's crackdown on PTI/Imran Khan supporters provides high-volume civilian traffic that operationally conceals militant communications.

▶ PTA Firewall Effect: China-style national firewall deployed in 2024 drives nationwide VPN demand, creating a high-baseline VPN environment that degrades signal-to-noise ratio for all law enforcement.

▌ 9. Intelligence Recommendations
[HIGH]

Priority Monitoring Zones

Focus active digital surveillance on Pulwama–Kulgam–Shopian (South Kashmir) and Doda–Kishtwar (Chenab Valley). Correlate VPN traffic spikes with incident timelines and known militant activity calendars. Establish dedicated monitoring cells at district SSP level in these zones.

Deep Packet Inspection Deployment

Deploy DPI at ISP level to detect VPN handshake patterns (OpenVPN, WireGuard, Shadowsocks, Obfs4). Even without payload decryption, destination IP blocks, connection timing, and session duration can flag suspicious encrypted sessions for further investigation.

Identify PoK-origin social media accounts operating via VPN with Indian-region IP spoofing. Correlate posting patterns, timing, and narratives with known Information Operations campaigns. Flag accounts showing J&K-origin posting behavior from PoK VPN exit nodes.

[MED]

App Store Intelligence — Early Warning

Monitor VPN app download spikes via I4C channel (Apple/Google data) correlated by district geolocation. Sudden surges in high-threat districts are tactical early warning indicators of impending militant activity or civil unrest windows.

[MED]

Signal-Noise Differentiation Protocol

Of 1,100+ identified, the majority are civilians. Establish a tiered response: Level 1 (warning + device check), Level 2 (security proceedings), Level 3 (FIR) — based on background check outcomes, VPN app characteristics, and behavioral indicators. Avoid blanket criminalisation of civilian use.

[LOW]

National VPN Policy Alignment

Advocate for resolution of the MoC–MeitY jurisdictional gap. A clear national VPN framework aligned with TRAI recommendations would provide more durable enforcement capability than district-level Section 163 emergency orders which are legally fragile and finite.

▌ 10. Conclusion

VPN usage in J&K is a documented, active threat vector being operationally exploited by militant actors for communication security, lawful interception evasion, and cross-LoC coordination. The enforcement data confirms meaningful threat presence in South Kashmir (Pulwama–Kulgam–Shopian) and Chenab Valley (Doda–Kishtwar). The administration's enforcement response, while operationally aggressive, requires a more targeted signal-differentiation methodology to maintain efficacy while minimising civilian impact.

In PoK/AJK, the threat landscape is assessed as HIGH across Muzaffarabad, Neelum, Rawalakot, and Mirpur — with convergence of militant operational use and mass civilian VPN adoption driven by Pakistan's internet censorship environment. The same digital tool that enables cross-LoC militant C2 is being used by millions of ordinary Pakistani and PoK citizens to access blocked social media — creating a structural challenge for signal intelligence extraction that no simple enforcement order can resolve.

▌ Frequently Asked Questions

Is VPN illegal in Jammu & Kashmir?

VPN use is not illegal under national Indian law. However, district magistrates in J&K have issued emergency prohibitory orders under Section 163 of the BNSS 2023, banning VPN use in 14 of 20 districts for a 2-month period from December 2025. Violators face security proceedings under Sections 126/170 BNSS, and in cases with terror-related antecedents, criminal FIRs under Section 223 BNS.

Which districts in J&K have VPN bans in 2026?

As of January 2026, VPN bans have been imposed in at least 14 districts: Pulwama, Kulgam, Shopian, Budgam, Kupwara, Srinagar, Baramulla, Bandipora, Ganderbal, Anantnag (all 10 Kashmir Valley districts) and Doda, Kishtwar, Rajouri, Poonch, Udhampur, and Kathua in the Jammu region.

How many people were identified for VPN use in Kashmir?

As of January 6, 2026, J&K Police identified over 1,100 individuals for using VPNs in violation of district prohibitory orders. On January 1, 2026 alone, over 130 were detained in the Kashmir Valley. Two FIRs were filed in Budgam against individuals with adverse terror-related antecedents confirmed through device forensics and technical analysis.

What is the VPN situation in Pakistan Occupied Kashmir (PoK/AJK)?

PoK/AJK is Pakistan-administered with no direct enumeration data. Based on PTA regulatory filings and open-source assessment, Muzaffarabad, Neelum Valley, Rawalakot, and Mirpur are rated ASSESSED HIGH for VPN usage — serving militant C2 communications, social media bypass (X/Twitter is blocked in Pakistan since Feb 2024), and diaspora financial communications.

What legal section governs the J&K VPN ban?

The bans are issued under Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) 2023 — the successor to Section 144 CrPC — which grants emergency powers to executive magistrates to issue urgent preventive orders. Enforcement uses Sections 126 and 170 BNSS (bound-down proceedings) and Section 223 BNS (criminal FIR where terror links are established).
