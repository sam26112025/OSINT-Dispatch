Cryptocurrency Tracing: OSINT Techniques for Following the Money

File No.IGRS-032/2026
SubjectOpen-source intelligence methodology for tracing cryptocurrency transactions in fraud investigations
CategoryCyber Fraud
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-05-26
Reading time7 minutes

Cryptocurrency's transparency is often misunderstood — blockchain transactions are public by design. How investigators trace funds through wallets, mixers, and exchanges.

The transparency paradox

Cryptocurrency is often described as anonymous, but most major blockchains — Bitcoin, Ethereum, and most layer-2 networks — are pseudonymous rather than anonymous. Every transaction is recorded permanently on a public ledger, visible to anyone, forever. The pseudonymity comes from addresses — random-looking strings with no inherent identity linkage. But addresses leave traces, cluster together through common usage patterns, and eventually touch real-world infrastructure — exchanges, payment processors, merchants — that must comply with KYC/AML regulations. This is where tracing converts pseudonymous addresses to real identities.

The blockchain as evidence

Every cryptocurrency transaction provides: the sending address, the receiving address, the amount, a timestamp, and the transaction hash (a unique identifier). For Bitcoin, the UTXO model additionally shows which previous transaction outputs funded the current transaction. This creates a complete, immutable graph of value flow that cannot be altered after the fact. For investigators, this is a forensic dream — evidence that cannot be destroyed, tampered with, or claimed to have been misrecorded.

Free OSINT tools for blockchain analysis

  • Blockchain.com explorer, Blockchair, Mempool.space (Bitcoin): Enter any address or transaction hash to see the full history. Blockchair additionally provides cluster analysis and basic entity identification.
  • Etherscan (Ethereum): Complete Ethereum transaction history with contract interaction details. Token transfers, DeFi interactions, and NFT transactions are all visible.
  • OSINT Framework blockchain section: Aggregates multiple free analysis tools by blockchain.
  • Chainalysis Reactor free tier / Crystal Blockchain: Commercial tools with free limited-use tiers; significantly more powerful for entity identification and pattern analysis.

Address clustering

The most powerful technique in free-tool blockchain OSINT is address clustering — identifying groups of addresses controlled by the same entity. Common-input ownership heuristic: when multiple addresses are inputs to the same transaction, they are typically controlled by the same wallet. Following the change output: Bitcoin transactions often produce a change output returning to the sender; identifying change outputs allows clustering. Over multiple transactions, a single entity's full address set often becomes visible, revealing the total funds controlled.

Exchange attribution

The terminal point of most crypto fraud investigations is an exchange — where cryptocurrency is converted to fiat. Major exchanges serving Indian users (WazirX, CoinDCX, Binance, OKX) are required under PMLA to maintain KYC records and respond to law enforcement legal process. When tracing leads to a deposit at an identified exchange, a Section 91 BNSS notice to that exchange (or, for foreign exchanges, a mutual legal assistance request via CBI) can produce the KYC identity behind the receiving address. This converts the blockchain's pseudonymous address into a name, phone number, and bank account.

Cryptocurrency mixing services (tumblers) and privacy coins (Monero) are designed to defeat tracing. However, even mixed funds must eventually touch real-world infrastructure — and the mixing service itself is often an identifiable entity that law enforcement can target.
