Ransomware Attack Response — Incident Response Guide for Indian Organizations

Ransomware attacks on Indian organizations increased 138% in 2025, targeting healthcare, manufacturing, education, and government sectors. This guide provides a structured incident response framework for Indian security teams.

Phase 1: Detection and Containment (0-2 hours)

  1. Isolate infected systems — disconnect from network immediately (pull ethernet, disable WiFi)
  2. Do NOT shut down — running memory may contain encryption keys
  3. Preserve memory — take RAM dump using Magnet RAM Capture or Volatility
  4. Identify patient zero — which system was first encrypted
  5. Check backups — are they intact and offline?

Phase 2: CERT-In Reporting (Mandatory)

Under CERT-In directions 2022 (amended 2023), ransomware attacks must be reported to CERT-In within 6 hours of detection. Report at:

  • Email: incident@cert-in.org.in
  • Phone: 1800-11-4949
  • Portal: cert-in.gov.in

Failure to report within 6 hours attracts penalties up to ₹1 lakh per day. Critical infrastructure organizations face higher penalties.

Phase 3: Forensic Investigation

  • Identify ransomware strain using ID Ransomware (id-ransomware.malwarehunterteam.com)
  • Check for decryptors at No More Ransom (nomoreransom.org)
  • Preserve encrypted files — do not delete
  • Review Windows Event Logs: Security log 4624 (logon), 4648 (explicit logon)
  • Check RDP logs, firewall logs for initial access vector

Phase 4: Legal Obligations

Under DPDP Act 2026, data breaches must be reported to the Data Protection Board within prescribed timelines. If customer/employee personal data was exfiltrated alongside encryption (double extortion), DPDP reporting is mandatory. Failure attracts penalties up to ₹200 crore.

Phase 5: Recovery Decision

Do NOT pay ransom as a first resort. Payment funds criminal operations and does not guarantee decryption. Steps before considering payment:

  1. Check No More Ransom for free decryptor
  2. Restore from offline backups
  3. Engage CERT-In for assistance
  4. Contact law enforcement (cybercrime.gov.in)

Frequently Asked Questions

What to do immediately after a ransomware attack in India?
Isolate infected systems from the network immediately. Do not shut down (RAM may contain keys). Preserve memory dump. Report to CERT-In within 6 hours (mandatory) at incident@cert-in.org.in. Check backups and identify the ransomware strain using ID Ransomware.
Is ransomware reporting mandatory in India?
Yes. Under CERT-In Directions 2022, ransomware attacks must be reported to CERT-In within 6 hours of detection. Failure attracts penalties. Organizations must also maintain logs for 180 days and provide them to CERT-In on demand.
Should you pay ransom in India?
Law enforcement agencies and CERT-In advise against paying ransom. Payment funds criminal operations, does not guarantee decryption, and may make you a repeat target. First check No More Ransom (nomoreransom.org) for free decryptors and restore from backups.
