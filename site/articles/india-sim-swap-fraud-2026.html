The SIM Swap Epidemic: How Indian Mobile Numbers Are Being Hijacked in 2026

File No.IGRS-014/2026
SubjectSIM swap fraud mechanics, telecom loopholes, and defensive countermeasures for Indian users
CategoryCyber Fraud
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-04-02
Reading time7 minutes

SIM swap attacks have surged across Indian metros. Attackers need only your Aadhaar number and a corrupt telecom agent to seize your number — and everything tied to it.

What SIM swapping actually is

A SIM swap is not a technical hack. It is a social engineering attack against a telecom provider. The attacker convinces the provider — through forged documents, insider assistance, or impersonation — to transfer your mobile number to a SIM card they control. The moment the transfer completes, your phone loses signal. Every call and SMS intended for you now goes to the attacker's device — including OTPs for banking, UPI, and email recovery.

The Indian threat landscape in 2026

India's telecom subscriber base exceeds 1.1 billion, making it one of the largest attack surfaces globally. Multiple operators still process SIM replacement requests at retail outlets with minimal verification. Dark-web markets openly advertise "SIM porting services" targeting specific Indian operators, with prices ranging from ₹2,000 to ₹15,000 depending on operator and turnaround.

Victims typically discover the attack when their phone displays "No SIM" or "No Service" — often in early morning hours when fraud helplines are least staffed. By the time a complaint is filed, funds have already moved through mule accounts.

Attack sequence

The attacker gathers your phone number, name, address, and Aadhaar from data broker sites or previous breaches. They visit a telecom outlet with forged documents or contact a complicit retailer. The replacement SIM is activated. Within minutes, password resets begin on email, UPI apps, and banking portals. Funds transfer to mule accounts within the first hour.

If your phone suddenly loses all signal, call your operator immediately from another phone. Simultaneously call 1930 and your primary bank. Every minute matters.

Defence

  • Replace SMS OTP with authenticator apps wherever the service permits — TOTP codes are not intercepted by SIM swaps
  • Set a SIM lock PIN with your operator — most carriers allow a secondary PIN before any replacement is processed
  • Use a separate number for banking — a number not published anywhere, used only for financial OTPs
  • Register alternate contact details with your operator for pre-change notifications

Legal recourse

SIM swap fraud is cognizable under Section 66C (identity theft) and Section 66D of the IT Act, 2000, and Section 318 of the BNS 2023. File an FIR at the cyber police station, report to 1930, and lodge a written complaint with your telecom operator to trigger an internal fraud investigation.
