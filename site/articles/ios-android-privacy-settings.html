Privacy Hardening for Android and iOS: The Settings That Actually Matter in 2026

File No.IGRS-056/2026
SubjectPractical privacy configuration guide for Android and iOS smartphones in 2026
CategoryPrivacy & Safety
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-05-24
Reading time5 minutes

Most privacy advice focuses on apps. The settings that matter most are built into the operating system itself. The complete list, explained.

Android: the critical settings

App permissions review: Settings → Privacy → Permission Manager. Review Location, Camera, Microphone, Contacts, Phone, and SMS for each app. Revoke any permission not needed for the app's core function. Pay particular attention to "Allow all the time" location access — few apps legitimately require it. Advertising ID: Settings → Privacy → Ads → Delete advertising ID. This removes the cross-app tracking identifier advertising networks use to build usage profiles. On Android 12 and later, you can delete it entirely. Google Activity Controls: myaccount.google.com → Data and Privacy — review and pause Web and App Activity, Location History, and YouTube History if you do not want Google building a browsing profile. Google Play Services location: Settings → Location → App permissions → Google Play Services — a hidden permission controlling whether Google can access location independently of any other app.

iOS: the critical settings

App Tracking Transparency: Settings → Privacy and Security → Tracking → Allow Apps to Request to Track → OFF. This prevents apps from even asking permission to track you across apps using the advertising identifier. Location permissions audit: Settings → Privacy and Security → Location Services — most apps should be "While Using" or "Never", not "Always". Navigation apps are the main legitimate exception. Precise location toggle: For each app with location access, the Precise Location toggle controls whether the app receives exact GPS or an approximate location — turn off for social media, shopping, and other apps that do not require precise location. App Privacy Report: Settings → Privacy and Security → App Privacy Report — shows which apps accessed your camera, microphone, contacts, and location, and which external domains they contacted, in the last seven days.

For both platforms: Regularly review installed apps and delete anything unused in the last 30 days. Apps you are not using still run background processes, collect data, and represent unnecessary attack surface. Fewer apps means meaningfully less exposure.
