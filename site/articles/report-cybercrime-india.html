How to Report Cybercrime in India: 1930, cybercrime.gov.in and What Happens Next

File No.OD-012/2026
SubjectCitizen's procedural guide to cybercrime reporting channels in India
CategoryCyber Awareness
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-06-10
Reading time6 minutes

Money gone from your account, a hacked profile, online harassment — the first hours decide the outcome. Exactly how to report cybercrime in India and what to expect afterward.

The two channels every Indian should memorise

  • Helpline 1930 — the national cyber fraud helpline, for financial fraud in progress. Operated under the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C), it connects to a system that can flag fraudulent transactions across banks and payment platforms and freeze funds mid-transit.
  • cybercrime.gov.in — the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal, for filing detailed complaints on all categories: financial fraud, hacking and account takeover, online harassment and stalking, sextortion, identity theft, and (with a dedicated anonymous track) content involving child sexual abuse.
If money has just left your account, stop reading and call 1930 now. Fund recovery works by freezing money as it hops between mule accounts — possible in the first hours, increasingly unlikely after the first day. Speed beats paperwork.

Reporting financial fraud: the sequence

  1. Call 1930 immediately with transaction details — amounts, time, the account/UPI ID or number money went to, your bank.
  2. Notify your bank through its official fraud line or app; ask for the disputed-transaction process and note the reference number. RBI rules limit customer liability for unauthorised transactions when reported promptly — the clock matters legally, not just practically.
  3. File the written complaint on cybercrime.gov.in the same day. You will receive an acknowledgement number; the complaint routes to your state's cyber cell.
  4. Follow up in person if needed. For serious amounts, visit your local cyber police station with ID and evidence; you are entitled to file a complaint regardless of where the fraudster is located.

What evidence to preserve (before it disappears)

  • Full-screen screenshots of fraudulent messages, profiles, listings and payment pages — including sender numbers/handles and visible dates.
  • Bank/UPI transaction records: IDs, timestamps, beneficiary details.
  • Call recordings or numbers used by fraudsters; emails with full headers where possible.
  • Do not delete the conversation, uninstall the app, or factory-reset the device — victims destroy their own evidence in the panic to "clean up". Preserve first, clean later.

Harassment, sextortion and account takeover

  • Online harassment/stalking: report on the portal (women and children can use the dedicated women/children category, which allows anonymous reporting for certain offences). Simultaneously use the platform's in-app reporting — platforms act on takedowns faster than legal process does.
  • Sextortion: do not pay — payment invites escalation, not deletion. Cease contact, preserve everything, report on the portal and to the platform. The StopNCII service can help block intimate images from being shared on participating platforms without you uploading the images themselves.
  • Hacked accounts: use the platform's account-recovery flow immediately, then secure your email (the master key), change passwords, enable two-factor authentication, and review connected apps and forwarding rules. File on the portal if impersonation or fraud followed the takeover.

Also worth knowing

  • Sanchar Saathi (sancharsaathi.gov.in): report fraud calls/SMS via its Chakshu facility, block your lost/stolen phone's IMEI, and check what SIM numbers are registered against your identity.
  • Suspect spoofed/violating SMS senders can also be reported to your telecom operator via 1909 (TRAI's DND framework).

What happens after you file

Your portal complaint generates an acknowledgement number and is assigned to the jurisdictional cyber cell, which may contact you for statements and original evidence. Track status on the portal. Honest expectations: freezes and recoveries in fast-reported financial fraud are common; cross-border prosecutions are slow. But reporting always matters — it feeds the pattern data that lets I4C and banks block mule accounts, fraud numbers and phishing domains at scale, protecting the next ten thousand targets even when an individual case moves slowly.
