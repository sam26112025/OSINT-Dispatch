India Ranked #1 Global Cyber Risk by WEF 2026 — 3,195 Attacks Per Week Per Organisation

By Manuj Kumar Pandey27 July 2026Threat IntelligenceWEF Risk #13195 Attacks/WeekCheck Point 2026

The World Economic Forum Global Risk Report 2026 has ranked cybersecurity as India's number one national risk — ahead of economic downturns, climate disasters, and armed conflict. Check Point Software's concurrent 2026 Cyber Security Report provides the operational data: Indian organisations face an average of 3,195 cyber attacks per week, with education institutions bearing the highest load at 7,684 attacks per week.

Why India is Most Targeted

India's rapid digitisation — 1.4 billion users, UPI processing billions of transactions monthly, Aadhaar-linked digital identity ecosystem — creates an unparalleled attack surface. Limited cybersecurity budgets in education and government, legacy systems, and a massive base of first-time internet users combine to create high-value, low-resistance targets for global cybercriminals.

CERT-In mandatory reporting: All cyber incidents must be reported to CERT-In within 6 hours of detection. Critical infrastructure operators must also notify NCIIPC. DPDP Act 2023 adds 72-hour mandatory breach notification to Data Protection Board once fully operationalised (May 2027).

Frequently Asked Questions

What does India's #1 WEF cyber risk ranking mean?
The WEF Global Risk Report 2026 ranks cybersecurity as India's most critical national risk — above economic recession, climate change, and armed conflict. This reflects India's massive digital attack surface, high frequency of attacks (3,195/week/org per Check Point), and the systemic risk posed by cybercrime to national infrastructure.
How many cyber attacks does India face weekly?
According to Check Point's 2026 report, Indian organisations face an average of 3,195 cyber attacks per week. Education institutions are the most targeted at 7,684 attacks per week per organisation. CERT-In logged 29.44 lakh incidents in 2025 alone.
What is CERT-In's incident reporting requirement?
Under CERT-In's 2022 Directions, all organisations must report cyber incidents within 6 hours of detection. Critical infrastructure operators must additionally notify NCIIPC. DPDP Act 2023 adds 72-hour breach notification to Data Protection Board of India.
What is driving India's cybersecurity crisis?
Three factors: (1) AI democratisation of attack tools — elite APT capabilities now accessible to script kiddies, (2) rapid digitisation creating massive attack surface with inadequate security investment, (3) high-value targets (UPI, Aadhaar, banking data) with relatively low organisational security maturity.
