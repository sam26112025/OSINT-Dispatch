How to File a Cybercrime FIR in India — Section by Section Guide 2026

Why Getting the FIR Right Matters in Cybercrime Cases

Filing a cybercrime FIR with the correct legal sections is the foundation on which an entire prosecution rests. Wrong or outdated sections lead to easy bail for the accused, inadmissible evidence, and cases that collapse in court. With the introduction of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) 2023 replacing the old IPC, CrPC, and Evidence Act, many investigators are still navigating the transition. This guide provides a complete, section-by-section reference for filing cybercrime FIRs in India in 2026.

Where to File a Cybercrime FIR

Cybercrime jurisdiction in India is deliberately flexible. Under the BNSS, a complaint can be filed where the victim resides, where the offence occurred, or where its consequences were felt. This means victims are never forced to travel to where the offender operates. The filing options are:

  • cybercrime.gov.in — mandatory first step for financial fraud, generates a complaint number
  • Local cyber cell or police station — for FIR registration
  • 1930 helpline — for rapid response to financial fraud

The Critical Mistake: Section 66A Is Dead

Section 66A of the IT Act was struck down by the Supreme Court in the landmark Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015) judgment for being unconstitutionally vague and violating free speech. Despite this, it is still occasionally — and wrongly — cited in FIRs. Using a struck-down provision is a serious error that can derail a case and invite judicial criticism. Section 66A must never appear in any FIR.

Evidence Admissibility Under BSA 2023

An FIR is only as strong as the evidence supporting it. Under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023, electronic evidence requires a Section 63 certificate (formerly Section 65B of the Evidence Act). This certificate authenticates the electronic record, describes how it was produced, confirms the system functioned properly, and includes the hash value. Without it, digital evidence — bank records, chats, logs — may be ruled inadmissible. Hash values (SHA-256) should be computed at the time of collection to prove integrity.

What to Do If Police Refuse to Register an FIR

Police are legally obligated to register an FIR for a cognizable offence. If they refuse, the victim has clear escalation options:

  • Submit a written complaint to the Superintendent of Police under Section 173 BNSS
  • Approach a Magistrate under Section 175 BNSS to direct registration
  • File the complaint online at cybercrime.gov.in, which is forwarded to the cyber cell
  • Document the refusal in writing for the record

The BNS, BNSS, BSA Transition Explained

Old LawNew Law (2023)Covers
Indian Penal Code (IPC)Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)Substantive offences
Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC)Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)Procedure

For offences committed after 1 July 2024, the new codes apply. For older offences during the transitional period, the previous codes may still govern. Investigators must apply the correct framework based on the date of the offence.

After the FIR: What Happens Next

Once registered, the FIR triggers formal investigation: collection of bank and telecom records via legal notice, money-trail analysis, suspect identification, and evidence assembly. The victim can track progress using the complaint or FIR number. Persistent, documented follow-up is often what keeps a case moving. A well-drafted FIR with correct sections, supported by properly preserved and certified evidence, gives the investigation the strongest possible foundation and dramatically improves the odds of conviction and recovery.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do I file a cybercrime FIR in India?
File online at cybercrime.gov.in for financial fraud (mandatory first step). Then approach your local police cyber cell or nearest police station. Cybercrime jurisdiction is flexible — you can file where you are located, where the server is, or where the offender is (Section 13 BNSS).
What documents do I need to file a cybercrime FIR?
Screenshots of all communications, bank statements with UTR numbers, phone numbers/email IDs of suspect, any URLs or domain names involved, and a written complaint narrating the incident chronologically.
Can I file a cybercrime FIR online in India?
Yes. Visit cybercrime.gov.in and register your complaint. You will receive a complaint number. For financial fraud, this is the mandatory first step. The complaint is then forwarded to your local cyber cell for FIR registration.
Which BNS section replaces IPC 420 for cybercrime?
Section 318 of BNS 2023 replaces IPC Section 420 (cheating). For offences after 1 July 2024, use BNS sections. For older cases, IPC sections may still apply during the transitional period.
