Digital Arrest Scam — How It Works and How to Investigate

Digital arrest scams have emerged as one of India's most psychologically devastating forms of cybercrime. The Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) has issued multiple advisories on this threat, and Prime Minister Narendra Modi addressed it in his Mann Ki Baat broadcast in October 2024. Despite widespread awareness campaigns, the scam continues to claim victims across all demographics.

What Is a Digital Arrest Scam?

A digital arrest scam involves criminals impersonating law enforcement officials — typically CBI, Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB), TRAI, or Customs officers — via video call. The victim is told they are under "digital arrest" for a fabricated crime (drug trafficking, money laundering, tax evasion, or involvement in a criminal case). They are then coerced into staying on the video call for hours or days while being extorted.

Modus Operandi — Step by Step

Phase 1: Initial Contact

The victim receives a call from an automated voice claiming to be from TRAI, a courier company, or a government department. The message states that the victim's phone number, Aadhaar, or courier package is linked to illegal activity. The call is then transferred to a "senior officer".

Phase 2: Escalation

A second caller, posing as a police officer or CBI agent, contacts the victim via WhatsApp or Skype video call. They display fake ID cards, fake arrest warrants, and a professional-looking "police station" background (often a rented room decorated with props). The victim is told they are under "digital arrest" and must not speak to anyone or disconnect the call.

Phase 3: Coercion

The fraudsters maintain psychological pressure through continuous surveillance via video call. They demand the victim transfer money to a "Supreme Court escrow account" or "RBI safety account" to prove their innocence. Multiple transactions are demanded over days, with each payment followed by a new "complication" requiring further payment.

Phase 4: Disappearance

Once the victim runs out of money or contacts a family member, the fraudsters disconnect and disappear. Phone numbers are abandoned, and the money is quickly distributed through mule accounts.

Red Flags for Victims

  • No legitimate law enforcement agency conducts arrests via video call
  • CBI, NCB, and police never demand money to "clear" a case
  • "Digital arrest" has no legal basis in Indian law
  • TRAI does not disconnect phone numbers for criminal activity — courts do
  • No government agency uses WhatsApp or Skype for official communication

Investigation Framework for Law Enforcement

Immediate Steps (0-24 hours)

When a complaint is received, the following immediate actions must be taken:

  • Register complaint on cybercrime.gov.in and obtain complaint number
  • Collect all transaction details — UTR numbers, account numbers, IFSC codes
  • Immediately contact the destination bank through the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal's bank nodal officer network to freeze mule accounts
  • Preserve WhatsApp chat history, call logs, and any email correspondence
  • Obtain a Section 91 BNSS (formerly CrPC 91) notice to the victim's bank for account statements

OSINT Investigation

  • Phone number OSINT — Truecaller, TAFCOP portal, telecom CDR request
  • WhatsApp number verification — check account creation date, profile photo metadata
  • Bank account trail — follow mule account chain using Razorpay/Paytm/bank nodal officers
  • IP address — if victim has call recording, extract metadata for IP forensics
  • Video call background analysis — reverse image search props and background

Evidence Preservation

For court admissibility under Section 63 BSA 2023, ensure the following:

  • Screenshot all communications with timestamp and hash value (SHA-256)
  • Obtain Section 63 BSA certificate from the platform/bank providing digital evidence
  • Preserve WhatsApp backup before factory reset
  • Record victim's statement immediately — psychological state degrades with time

Victim Resources

  • National Cyber Crime Helpline: 1930
  • Report online: cybercrime.gov.in
  • Bank fraud: Contact RBI Ombudsman if bank refuses to freeze/reverse
  • MHA advisory: cybercrime.gov.in/Home/Cybersafe

Conclusion

Digital arrest scams succeed through psychological manipulation and impersonation of trusted authority. For investigators, the first 24 hours are critical — account freezing before money mule dispersal is the only realistic chance of recovery. Victim sensitivity is paramount; these cases involve significant psychological trauma and financial loss, often affecting elderly and first-time internet users.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a digital arrest scam?
A digital arrest scam is a cybercrime where fraudsters impersonate law enforcement officials (CBI, NCB, TRAI) via video call and claim the victim is under 'digital arrest' for fabricated crimes, then extort money. There is no legal basis for 'digital arrest' in Indian law.
How do I report a digital arrest scam in India?
Call the National Cyber Crime Helpline 1930 immediately, or file a complaint online at cybercrime.gov.in. Collect all evidence — screenshots, call recordings, bank transaction UTR numbers — before reporting.
Can police really arrest you on a video call?
No. No legitimate law enforcement agency in India conducts arrests via video call. CBI, NCB, police, and TRAI never demand money to 'clear' a case or conduct 'digital arrests'. If you receive such a call, disconnect immediately and report to 1930.
What legal sections apply to digital arrest scam?
Section 318(4) BNS (cheating), Section 308 BNS (extortion), Section 319 BNS (cheating by personation), Section 66C IT Act (identity theft), Section 66D IT Act (cheating by personation using computer), and Section 170 BNS (impersonating a public servant).
