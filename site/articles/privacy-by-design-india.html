Privacy by Design Under India's DPDP Act: What Organizations Must Build In

File No.IGRS-061/2026
SubjectImplementation guide for Privacy by Design under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023
CategoryLaw & Evidence
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-06-07
Reading time5 minutes

India's DPDP Act requires privacy to be built into systems from the start, not bolted on later. What this means in practice for Indian product teams and technology organizations.

The DPDP Act's design obligation

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 establishes Privacy by Design as a core principle — privacy protections incorporated into systems during their design phase, not added as an afterthought after build. While the Act's Rules are still being finalized, the principle is established in law and the Data Protection Board will assess compliance against it. Organizations that wait for the Rules before beginning implementation will find themselves significantly behind when enforcement starts.

What Privacy by Design means in practice

Data minimisation: Collect only what is genuinely necessary for the stated purpose — every additional field in a form is a design decision requiring explicit justification. The default posture should be to not collect, unless necessity is demonstrated. Purpose limitation: Design systems so that data collected for one purpose cannot easily be repurposed — separate data stores with separate access controls implement this technically. Storage limitation: Build automatic deletion into data systems — set retention periods and enforce them through technical controls, not administrative procedures that are easily overridden or forgotten. Access controls: Design systems so that employees access only the personal data their role genuinely requires — role-based access control is the technical implementation of this principle.

Data Protection Impact Assessments

DPIAs are the primary practical tool for implementing Privacy by Design. Conduct a DPIA for every new system or significant change to existing systems that involves personal data processing. The process of identifying privacy risks before building systematically drives privacy considerations into design decisions. A DPIA template calibrated to DPDP Act requirements provides the structure for this analysis.

Organizations that wait for DPDP Rules to be finalized before beginning Privacy by Design implementation will find themselves significantly behind when enforcement begins. Start now with data minimisation and access control reviews — these are unambiguously required regardless of Rule specifics and also represent good security practice independently of compliance.
