Spear Phishing Defence: Why Targeted Attacks Succeed and How to Stop Them

Generic phishing filters don't stop spear phishing. Targeted attacks succeed because they're built from OSINT research. Understanding how they're constructed is the key to defeating them.

Spear Phishing: Targeted Deception

While ordinary phishing casts a wide net, spear phishing targets specific individuals with personalised, researched messages that are far harder to detect. These tailored attacks are behind many major breaches and business email compromise frauds. For Indian organisations and individuals in sensitive roles, understanding and defending against spear phishing is critical. This guide covers how it works and how to defend.

What Makes Spear Phishing Different

Spear phishing is distinguished by personalisation and research. Rather than generic mass emails, the attacker studies the target — their role, relationships, and habits — and crafts a message that is contextually convincing. It may appear to come from a known colleague, reference real projects, or exploit current circumstances. This specificity makes spear phishing dramatically more effective and harder to recognise than ordinary phishing.

The Role of OSINT

Spear phishing is powered by reconnaissance. Attackers gather information from LinkedIn, social media, company websites, and data breaches to understand their target and craft believable messages. The more an attacker knows — reporting structure, ongoing projects, personal interests — the more convincing the approach. This connection means an organisation's and individual's public information directly fuels the attacks against them.

Business Email Compromise

The most financially damaging form of spear phishing is business email compromise (BEC), where attackers impersonate executives or vendors to trick employees into transferring money or data. These attacks often involve no malware — just convincing deception exploiting authority and urgency. BEC has caused enormous losses to Indian businesses, making it a priority threat that technical filters alone cannot stop, since the emails are clean.

Recognising Spear Phishing

Because spear phishing is personalised, recognition requires vigilance: unexpected requests involving money or sensitive data, pressure to act urgently or bypass procedures, slight inconsistencies in sender addresses, and anything that discourages verification. The key habit is treating any sensitive request — however authoritative it appears — as warranting independent verification through a separate, trusted channel before acting.

Defending Against Spear Phishing

Effective defence is layered:

  • Verification procedures for financial and sensitive requests
  • Email authentication (DMARC, DKIM, SPF) to reduce spoofing
  • Security awareness training with simulated spear phishing
  • Limiting publicly available information that aids reconnaissance
  • Technical controls flagging external and suspicious emails
  • A culture where verifying unusual requests is encouraged

Building Resilience

For Indian organisations, spear phishing and BEC represent a severe and growing threat that exploits people rather than technology. Building resilience means combining technical controls like email authentication with human defences — verification habits, awareness, and a culture that supports caution. The single most protective measure is independent verification of sensitive requests, which defeats the urgency and authority that spear phishing depends on. In an environment where these targeted attacks cause major financial losses, investing in both the technical and human elements of defence is essential.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is spear phishing?
Spear phishing is a targeted phishing attack tailored to a specific individual or organization, using personalized information to appear legitimate. Unlike mass phishing, it researches the target to increase success rates.
How is spear phishing different from regular phishing?
Regular phishing casts a wide net with generic messages, while spear phishing targets specific people with personalized, researched content. Spear phishing is harder to detect and more likely to succeed against its chosen victims.
How do you defend against spear phishing?
Defend through security awareness, email authentication (SPF/DKIM/DMARC), verifying unusual requests via separate channels, scrutinizing sender details, hovering over links, and deploying advanced email filtering and anti-impersonation tools.
