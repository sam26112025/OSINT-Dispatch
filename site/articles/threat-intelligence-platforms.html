Threat Intelligence Platforms: MISP, OpenCTI, and Building a TI Capability on a Budget

File No.IGRS-051/2026
SubjectGuide to open-source threat intelligence platforms for Indian security teams with limited budgets
CategoryThreat Intelligence
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-05-09
Reading time6 minutes

Most Indian security teams cannot afford commercial TI platforms costing thousands of dollars per month. Open-source alternatives provide most of the capability at zero licensing cost.

What a TI platform does

A Threat Intelligence Platform (TIP) aggregates indicators from multiple feeds, stores intelligence in structured format, enables sharing with peer organizations, integrates with detection and response tooling to operationalize intelligence, and supports the analyst workflow of creating and disseminating intelligence products. Commercial TIPs from Recorded Future, ThreatConnect, and Anomali cost tens of thousands of dollars annually. Open-source alternatives provide most essential functionality.

MISP

MISP (Malware Information Sharing Platform) is the most widely deployed open-source TIP globally, used by hundreds of organizations including national CERTs. It provides: structured storage of indicators and threat events using a flexible taxonomy; bi-directional sharing with the global MISP network; REST API for SIEM, EDR, and firewall integration; support for STIX and multiple intelligence formats; and a web interface for analyst workflows. Deployable on-premises maintaining full data sovereignty — appropriate for sensitive investigative environments.

OpenCTI

OpenCTI, developed by ANSSI (France's national cybersecurity agency), emphasises relationship-based intelligence — storing not just indicators but the full context of threat actor campaigns, TTPs, and entity relationships. Its knowledge graph approach is particularly powerful for APT tracking and campaign analysis. Natively supports STIX 2.1, integrates with MITRE ATT&CK, and provides connectors to dozens of intelligence sources.

Building a functional TI capability from zero

Practical starting stack for an Indian security team: MISP deployed on-premises; free feed integration from Abuse.ch (URLhaus, MalwareBazaar, Feodo Tracker), AlienVault OTX, CERT-In advisories; SIEM integration to automatically alert on ingested IOCs; structured analyst workflow for creating internal intelligence products from incident findings.

Join the CERT-In information sharing community and sector-specific ISACs relevant to your industry. Peer sharing of indicators — particularly early indicators of campaigns targeting your sector — is among the highest-value TI activities and costs nothing.
