Threats to India's Critical Infrastructure: The Intelligence Picture in 2026

File No.IGRS-059/2026
SubjectOpen-source intelligence assessment of cyber threats targeting Indian critical infrastructure sectors
CategoryThreat Intelligence
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-06-02
Reading time6 minutes

India's power grid, financial system, telecom networks, and government services face persistent cyber threats. What the public intelligence record shows about the current threat landscape.

What the public record shows

India's critical infrastructure has been targeted by a range of threat actors over the past several years, from opportunistic ransomware affecting hospital operations to more sophisticated intrusions targeting energy and telecommunications infrastructure. The 2020 power grid incident in Mumbai — investigated by Recorded Future and attributed to Chinese state-linked actors — was the most prominent public example of sustained, patient targeting of Indian critical infrastructure by sophisticated threat actors with geopolitical motivation.

The sectors under pressure

Power and energy: Grid SCADA and operational technology systems are persistent targets. OT environments in Indian utilities often run legacy systems with limited security monitoring and incomplete air-gapping. The convergence of IT and OT networks for monitoring and efficiency purposes creates pathways from compromised corporate networks to operational systems. Financial sector: Banks, payment processors, and UPI infrastructure are high-value targets for financially motivated actors. Bank-facing fraud campaigns targeting employees with spearphishing remain high-volume. Telecom: India's backbone carries both civilian and government traffic, making it a priority intelligence target for nation-state actors.

The threat actor landscape

The public intelligence record identifies: nation-state aligned actors from China and Pakistan attributed with varying confidence to campaigns targeting Indian government and infrastructure; ransomware-as-a-service operators including LockBit affiliates and Royal that have affected Indian healthcare, manufacturing, and logistics; and hacktivist groups periodically targeting Indian government web infrastructure during geopolitical tensions, particularly around border incidents and diplomatic events.

This assessment is based entirely on public information — vendor reports, government advisories, and published research. It reflects what is known from open sources, not the full classified picture held by NTRO, RAW, and IB. The actual threat is broader than what is publicly documented.

The defensive gap

CERT-In's empanelled security auditor framework and the National Cyber Security Policy provide regulatory structures for critical infrastructure security. The implementation gap is significant: many critical infrastructure operators have not completed required baseline assessments, OT security skills are scarce, and the 6-hour CERT-In incident reporting requirement is inconsistently met outside the financial sector.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main cyber threats to India's critical infrastructure?
India's critical infrastructure faces threats from state-sponsored APTs, ransomware targeting power and healthcare, supply chain attacks, and attacks on financial and telecom systems. Power grids, ports, and banking are high-value targets.
How does India protect critical infrastructure from cyberattacks?
India protects critical infrastructure through NCIIPC (National Critical Information Infrastructure Protection Centre), CERT-In directives, mandatory incident reporting within 6 hours, sector-specific CERTs, and security audits of protected systems.
What is NCIIPC in India?
NCIIPC is the National Critical Information Infrastructure Protection Centre, the nodal agency for protecting India's critical information infrastructure across sectors like power, banking, telecom, transport, and government services from cyber threats.
