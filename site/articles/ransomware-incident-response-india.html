Ransomware Incident Response: A Practical Playbook for Indian Organizations

File No.IGRS-020/2026
SubjectStep-by-step ransomware IR guide calibrated for Indian regulatory and operational context
CategoryIncident Response
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-04-20
Reading time7 minutes

Ransomware attacks on Indian organizations have tripled in the past two years. The first 48 hours of response determine whether the organization recovers cleanly or pays.

The Indian ransomware landscape

India has seen a dramatic increase in ransomware incidents targeting manufacturing, healthcare, logistics, and financial services organizations. Groups including LockBit affiliates, Royal, and several India-specific threat actors have been active. Indian organizations are particularly targeted because many combine sensitive data with security maturity that lags their sector globally, and because the regulatory environment — while tightening — has historically imposed lower penalties for breaches than European or North American equivalents.

Hour 0–4: Contain and preserve

The moment ransomware is confirmed, the priority is stopping lateral movement — not recovering encrypted files. Immediately isolate affected systems from the network by disconnecting ethernet cables and disabling Wi-Fi adapters. Do not shut systems down — in-memory forensic artefacts are valuable and shutdown may trigger additional encryption or wiping. Disable VPN access for all remote users. Identify the patient zero — the initial infection point — by checking the earliest encryption timestamp and working backward through logs.

Simultaneously, preserve everything available: network flow logs, DNS query logs, proxy logs, EDR telemetry, and any email logs that may contain the initial phishing vector. These logs have short retention windows on many systems and are critical for the subsequent forensic investigation and insurance claim.

Hour 4–24: Assess scope and notify

Map which systems are encrypted, which are clean, and which may be infected but not yet triggered. Check backup integrity immediately — many ransomware operators deliberately target backups in the days before triggering encryption. If backups are online and uncompromised, recovery options are vastly better. Assess whether data was exfiltrated before encryption — most modern ransomware groups operate "double extortion," threatening to publish stolen data if ransom is not paid. Look for evidence of data staging (large compressed archives, unusual outbound transfer volumes in the days prior).

CERT-In notification: Under the CERT-In directions of April 2022, ransomware incidents must be reported to CERT-In within 6 hours of detection. Failure to report carries penalties. Keep the notification concise and factual — do not speculate about attribution at this stage.

The ransom decision

Paying ransomware is not illegal in India, but it is strongly discouraged by CERT-In and law enforcement. Payment does not guarantee decryption, does not prevent data publication, and directly funds future attacks. Before considering payment, exhaust all options: check NoMoreRansom.org for free decryptors for your ransomware variant, verify backup integrity fully, and engage a specialist incident response firm who may have access to threat actor decryptors through prior negotiations.

Recovery and post-incident

Recovery should proceed from verified clean backups to isolated environments, with systems returning to production only after forensic confirmation they are clean. The root cause must be identified and remediated before anything returns to the network — re-infection during recovery is common. Conduct a full post-incident review, update incident response plans, and implement the defensive improvements identified. If data was confirmed exfiltrated, notify affected individuals per DPDP Act 2023 requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the first steps after a ransomware attack in India?
Isolate infected systems immediately without shutting down (to preserve RAM), report to CERT-In within 6 hours, identify the ransomware strain, check backups, and engage your incident response team. Do not pay ransom as a first resort.
Is ransomware reporting mandatory in India?
Yes. CERT-In directions require reporting ransomware and other cyber incidents within 6 hours of detection. Organizations must also retain logs for 180 days and cooperate with CERT-In investigations.
Should Indian organizations pay ransomware demands?
CERT-In and law enforcement advise against paying. Payment funds crime, doesn't guarantee recovery, and invites repeat attacks. First check No More Ransom for free decryptors and restore from offline backups.
