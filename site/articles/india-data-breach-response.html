Data Breach Response in India: CERT-In, DPDP, and What Organizations Must Do

File No.IGRS-042/2026
SubjectRegulatory compliance guide for data breach notification under Indian law in 2026
CategoryIncident Response
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-04-12
Reading time6 minutes

India's breach notification obligations have become significantly stricter. What organizations must report, to whom, within what timeframe, and what penalties apply for failure.

The regulatory framework

Indian organizations now operate under two overlapping breach notification regimes. CERT-In Directions of April 2022 impose a mandatory 6-hour notification window for cybersecurity incidents including data breaches. The DPDP Act 2023, once its Rules are finalized, will additionally require notification to the Data Protection Board and affected data principals.

CERT-In: the 6-hour requirement

The 6-hour clock starts when an organization becomes aware of a reportable incident — a deliberately broad trigger. Reportable types include unauthorized access, data breaches, ransomware, website defacement, and malware propagation affecting critical systems. Notification via CERT-In's portal or email need not be complete — submit what is known, with updates as investigation progresses.

What the initial notification must contain

Incident description; detection date and time; systems or data types affected (estimated if precise count unavailable); whether the incident is ongoing or contained; and immediate containment actions taken. CERT-In may request additional information and dispatch personnel in serious cases.

Penalties: Failure to report to CERT-In carries penalties under the IT Act. The DPDP Act's Data Protection Board can impose penalties up to Rs 250 crore for failure to notify a breach. Enforcement is increasing.

Building a breach response capability

Required capabilities: a documented incident response plan including breach notification procedures; a designated CERT-In contact point; pre-drafted notification templates for CERT-In, the Data Protection Board, and affected individuals; legal counsel on retainer familiar with both regimes; and regular tabletop exercises including breach notification scenarios under time pressure.
