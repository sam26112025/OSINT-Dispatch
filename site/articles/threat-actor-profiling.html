Threat Actor Profiling: Building Intelligence on Adversaries

File No.IGRS-041/2026
SubjectMethodology for threat actor research, attribution assessment, and profile construction
CategoryThreat Intelligence
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-04-09
Reading time6 minutes

Knowing who is targeting your organization — tools, techniques, motivation, history — fundamentally changes how you defend. How threat actor profiles are built and used.

Why attribution matters for defence

Defenders who know which threat actor is targeting them make significantly better decisions. Different actors use different TTPs — knowing the actor lets you prioritise defences against their specific techniques rather than defending equally against everything. It shapes intelligence requirements and calibrates the response — a nation-state APT requires different escalation than a ransomware affiliate.

Sources for threat actor research

Primary sources: vendor research reports (Mandiant, CrowdStrike, Recorded Future, Group-IB, Innefu Labs); government advisories (CERT-In, NCSC UK, CISA); academic security research; and MITRE ATT&CK Groups database (free, comprehensive, with TTP mappings). For actors targeting Indian organizations, CERT-In advisories and Indian security researchers are most operationally relevant.

Building a profile

A useful threat actor profile contains: common name and aliases across vendor naming conventions; attributed nation-state sponsor or financial motivation; primary target sectors and geographies; initial access methods; post-exploitation tooling and malware families; infrastructure patterns; historical campaign timeline; and confidence assessment of each element (high confidence from multiple independent sources vs single-source inference).

MITRE ATT&CK mapping

Mapping known TTPs to MITRE ATT&CK immediately translates threat intelligence into detections. If an actor uses T1053 (Scheduled Tasks) for persistence, monitor Windows Event ID 4698 in your environment. ATT&CK Navigator visualizes actor TTPs against your detection coverage, identifying gaps needing priority attention.

Attribution confidence levels matter. Saying "this activity resembles APT28 TTPs" differs from "this is APT28 with high confidence confirmed by government attribution." Apply the same epistemic rigour to threat actor reporting that you would to any intelligence claim.
