APT36 & SideCopy: Pakistan-Linked Groups Target India's Defence and Government Networks in 2026

By Manuj Kumar Pandey27 July 2026Threat IntelligenceAPT36 PakistanSideCopyDefence Networks

Seqrite's India Cyber Threat Report 2026 documents a coordinated hybrid warfare campaign blending APT36 (Transparent Tribe) and SideCopy attacks targeting India's defence, government, and critical infrastructure. Both groups are believed to be Pakistan-linked, operating with long-term espionage objectives.

Critical pattern: Both groups exploit India's digital government transition — spear phishing with Aadhaar-themed and tax-return-themed lures are primary initial access against government/defence personnel.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is APT36?
APT36 (Transparent Tribe) is Pakistan-linked, targeting Indian defence/government with Crimson RAT, CapraRAT Android malware, and spear phishing via government-themed lures.
What is SideCopy?
SideCopy is a Pakistan-linked sub-group of Transparent Tribe. In 2026 it uses MSI installers and DLL sideloading targeting Indian Army/CRPF/DRDO employees, particularly via fake Kavach-themed Android apps.
How to detect these attacks?
Monitor for .docm/.xlsm execution, suspicious DLL loading patterns, anomalous network traffic to newly registered domains, and unusual Android app installations on work devices.
Where to report APT activity in India?
NCIIPC for critical infrastructure. CERT-In for all cyber incidents. Ministry of Defence networks: NTRO/DIARA channels.
