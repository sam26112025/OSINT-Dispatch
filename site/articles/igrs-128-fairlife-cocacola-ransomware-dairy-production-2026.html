Fairlife (Coca-Cola) Ransomware July 2026: US Dairy Production Halted by Cyberattack

By Manuj Kumar Pandey27 July 2026Global Cyber IntelligenceFood Supply AttackProduction HaltedCritical Sector

Fairlife, a wholly owned Coca-Cola subsidiary and major US dairy manufacturer, was forced to temporarily halt milk production and operations in the United States in July 2026 following a ransomware attack. The attack demonstrates the expanding scope of ransomware beyond IT systems into operational technology (OT) — directly stopping physical production lines.

Incident Summary

ParameterDetail
CompanyFairlife LLC — wholly owned Coca-Cola subsidiary
IndustryFood manufacturing / dairy
ImpactUS milk production halted temporarily
Attack typeRansomware (group not publicly confirmed)
OT impactProduction systems directly affected — physical operations disrupted

Critical trend: Ransomware groups are increasingly targeting operational technology (OT) — production lines, SCADA systems, and industrial control systems — not just IT networks. When OT is hit, physical consequences follow: production stops, supply chains break.

India Food Sector Relevance

India's food processing sector (including major dairy cooperatives like Amul, Mother Dairy, and multinational food manufacturers) faces the same OT vulnerability pattern. FSSAI-regulated facilities with digitised production systems are increasingly targeted as ransomware groups discover that food supply chain disruption creates more payment pressure than IT outages alone.

For Indian food/dairy sector: CERT-In's OT/ICS security advisories specifically address industrial control system protection. Air-gap critical production systems from IT networks. NCIIPC has critical infrastructure protection mandates for food sector entities designated as CII operators.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Fairlife Coca-Cola ransomware attack?
Fairlife, a Coca-Cola subsidiary and major US dairy manufacturer, had milk production halted by a ransomware attack in July 2026. The attack directly affected operational technology (OT) systems — physical production lines, not just IT systems.
What is the OT/ransomware trend?
Ransomware groups increasingly target operational technology (OT) systems — SCADA, production control systems, industrial automation — because physical disruption creates more payment pressure than IT outages. Food, energy, and manufacturing are prime targets.
What is India's OT cybersecurity risk?
India's dairy cooperatives (Amul, Mother Dairy), food processors, and manufacturers with digitised production systems face the same OT vulnerability. NCIIPC designates critical food infrastructure. CERT-In has published OT/ICS-specific advisories.
How should Indian food manufacturers protect OT?
Air-gap production systems from IT networks, apply CERT-In's ICS security guidelines, conduct OT-specific vulnerability assessments, implement network segmentation between IT and OT zones, and register with NCIIPC for critical infrastructure protection.
