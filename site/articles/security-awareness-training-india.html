Building Effective Security Awareness in Indian Organizations

File No.IGRS-062/2026
SubjectFramework for security awareness programs that change behaviour rather than just deliver information
CategoryCyber Awareness
AuthorManuj Kumar Pandey
Published2026-06-09
Reading time5 minutes

Most security awareness training doesn't work. Understanding why — and what does work — based on behavioural science and operational experience in Indian organizations.

The problem with current training

The standard Indian organizational security awareness program: an annual e-learning module, a few posters about password security, and an email reminding staff not to click suspicious links. Evidence that this changes behaviour is weak. People complete the module, pass the quiz, and promptly fall for phishing tests at nearly the same rate as before the training. The reason is not that employees are unintelligent — it is that information transfer alone does not change behaviour under time pressure, ambiguity, and competing priorities.

What actually changes behaviour

Psychological safety for reporting: Employees who fear blame for falling for phishing do not report suspicious emails — they hope the problem disappears. Organizations where reporting is celebrated have significantly better detection rates. Immediate, relevant feedback: When a simulated phishing test catches an employee, the most effective response is immediate training showing exactly why that specific email was suspicious — not a generic module deployed months later. Repetition and variety: Monthly phishing simulations with varied pretexts build the recognition pattern over time that a single annual training cannot. Role-specific content: Finance team training should focus on payment fraud; HR on job application malware; executives on targeted spearphishing and "digital arrest" scams.

Practical program design

Minimum effective program for a mid-sized Indian organization: monthly phishing simulations using India-relevant pretexts (KYC expiry, GSTIN update, Aadhaar linking) with immediate educational feedback for those who click; a quarterly tabletop exercise for security and management covering incident scenarios; a clear, simple reporting mechanism with a committed response SLA; and a published policy that explicitly protects reporters from blame. GoPhish is open-source and free — providing phishing simulation infrastructure at zero licensing cost.

The most important metric is not click rate on simulations — it is reporting rate. An organization where 80% of employees report suspicious emails is significantly more resilient than one with a low simulation click rate if the employees are not actively reporting real threats. Measure what matters.
