Why Investigators Use Browser-Based Forensic Tools

Every utility on this page runs entirely inside your browser. Nothing you paste — no hash, no token, no extract of evidence — is ever uploaded to a server. For an investigator handling sensitive or legally protected material, that distinction matters: client-side processing preserves the integrity of the artefact, avoids exposing case data to a third party, and supports the compartmentalisation that operational security demands.

When each tool fits an investigation

Cryptographic hashing (MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256, SHA-512) is used to verify that a seized file has not changed and to match a sample against known-good or known-bad reference sets. Base64 and hex decoders unwrap obfuscated payloads, configuration strings, and email attachments. The CIDR calculator scopes a network range when you are mapping an organisation’s address space. The JWT decoder exposes the claims inside a session token during application analysis. The regex extractor — tuned for Indian identifiers such as Aadhaar, PAN, IFSC, and mobile numbers — pulls structured entities out of bulk text for identification or redaction, and the epoch converter aligns log timestamps when reconstructing a timeline.

A note on evidence handling

Record the hash values you generate directly in your case notes; a documented hash is the simplest way to prove later that an exhibit is unaltered. Because these tools never transmit data, they are well suited to work that must remain on a single controlled device. Where your findings may support an FIR or court submission, pair them with a certificate under Section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, which governs the admissibility of electronic evidence in India.
