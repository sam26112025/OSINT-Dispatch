Understanding Indicators of Compromise

An Indicator of Compromise (IOC) is a piece of forensic data — an IP address, a domain, a URL, a file hash, or an email address — that suggests a system may have been targeted or breached. IOCs are the common currency of threat intelligence: they let defenders detect, block, and hunt for activity that has been seen elsewhere, and they let investigators attribute and connect incidents.

Not all indicators are equal

The practical value of an indicator depends on how hard it is for an adversary to change. A file hash is trivial to alter by recompiling, so it ages quickly; an IP address or domain takes more effort to rotate; and the underlying tools, techniques, and procedures behind a campaign are the hardest of all to change. This idea — often called the Pyramid of Pain — explains why mature defence focuses less on chasing individual hashes and more on detecting durable behaviour.

Acting on an indicator in the Indian context

When you confirm a malicious indicator, the response is to block it at your perimeter, hunt retrospectively through your logs for earlier contact, and where appropriate report it. In India, suspected intrusions can be reported to CERT-In, and financial fraud should be escalated immediately through the 1930 helpline and the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal, where rapid reporting can freeze mule accounts before funds are dispersed. Always preserve the indicator and its context as a timestamped record for your case file.
