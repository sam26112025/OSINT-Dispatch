How investigators read an IP profile

A raw IP address is just an endpoint — the intelligence is in what sits behind it. This tool resolves the network ownership and behavioural signals that matter in an investigation.

ASN & ownership

The Autonomous System Number (ASN) identifies the network operator. Knowing whether an IP belongs to an ISP, a hosting provider, an educational, government, or banking network tells you a lot about expected traffic. A consumer fraud lead coming from a hosting ASN, for example, is often a sign of automation or anonymisation rather than a genuine residential user.

Anonymisation flags

The VPN, proxy, Tor and datacenter flags indicate whether the real origin is likely being masked. The abuser flag and abuser score reflect how much malicious traffic has historically originated from that network — useful for triaging the reputation of a source.

Geolocation caveat

IP geolocation is reliable at the country level and often at the city level, but it is an estimate, not a precise address. Treat it as supporting context, not standalone proof, especially in evidentiary contexts.
