■ Reporting Portals & Helplines
📞 1930 — National Cybercrime Helpline
1930
Call 1930 immediately after any financial cybercrime. System connects to CFCFRMS and can freeze mule accounts within minutes. Available 24/7. Most effective if called within 1 hour of fraud.
🌐 National Cybercrime Reporting Portal
cybercrime.gov.in
Online complaint registration for all types of cybercrime. Financial crimes, social media abuse, online content crimes. Track complaint status online. Complaints forwarded to jurisdictional police.
Report at cybercrime.gov.in ↗
🔒 CERT-In Incident Reporting
incident@cert-in.org.in
Mandatory incident reporting for organizations under IT Act. Report within 6 hours of detection. For cybersecurity incidents including ransomware, data breach, unauthorized access. Toll-free: 1800-11-4949.
cert-in.org.in ↗
📷 Report Fake Social Media
cybercrime.gov.in
Report fake profiles impersonating public figures, women harassment, morphed images, cyberbullying. Dedicated section for women and children at the cybercrime portal. Anonymous reporting available.
💻 TRAI SPAM Reporting
DND / Sanchar Saathi
Report unsolicited commercial calls and SMS. TRAI DND registry. Use Sanchar Saathi portal to report telecom fraud and check your registered mobile connections.
sancharsaathi.gov.in ↗
💰 RBI Ombudsman — Banking Fraud
cms.rbi.org.in
If bank refuses to refund unauthorized transaction, escalate to RBI Banking Ombudsman. File complaint online. Bank must respond within 21 days or RBI can direct compensation.
cms.rbi.org.in ↗

Reading India’s Cybercrime Numbers

Aggregate statistics are most useful when read as a map of where harm is concentrated rather than as a scoreboard. In India, the dominant themes of recent years are financial: UPI and payment fraud, the ‘digital arrest’ impersonation scams that coerce victims over video calls, and the mule-account networks that move stolen funds out of reach within minutes. Understanding the mechanics behind each category is what turns a headline figure into something an investigator or a defender can act on.

Two structural factors shape the data. First, reporting is improving — the 1930 helpline and the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal have made it easier to register complaints, so rising numbers partly reflect better visibility rather than only rising crime. Second, speed is decisive: because fraud proceeds are layered through mule accounts almost immediately, the window in which reporting can freeze and recover funds is measured in hours. These realities, more than any single statistic, explain where prevention and response effort should be directed.
